Financial system of India

The financial system of any country is one of the most important tool for the regime to enforce its dominance over the people. Therefore governments ensure that the currency controlled by them (in this case Rupees controlled and circulated by the Reserve Bank Of India) remains the de facto mode of exchange.

This is especially evident by the aggressive laws against crypto currency, exorbetant taxes and mandatory kyc that defeats the anonymity achived by crypto in the first place. Crypto's are in fact the worst nightmare of any government unless controlled by them because crypto currencies such a Monero(XMR) are inherently impossible to be controlled by any organisation no matter how much resources they possess. The users enjoy true freedom and anonymity while offloading dependency on fiat currency. People often argue that cryptos are not real but in fact they are more real than any fiat currency. In case of the fiat currency, the government has to maintain an illusion that the paper money they print has value whereas in case of crypto currency, you have to spend energy to mine and secure the network.Crypto is dependent on the thermodynamic laws in a way which is quiet beautiful and everyone should obtain and use them without kyc.

Money has value because everyone belives that it is valubale. If everyone became aware of this and actively rejected the idea of value to money the financial system can no longer function. In today's reality the financial system has become truely centralised with computers. The money has been reduced to mere data. It is data that resides in our virtual bank accounts not the money. In fact bank does not even have enough money at it at any give time. If everyone went to bank at the same time to withdraw their money the banking systems would instantly crash.

The probelm with UPI

UPI has its great benefits: the conveniance, security and ease of transactions at a large scale but due to its inherently centralised and proprietary nature, various problems arise.

Firstly the power and leverage of third parties. UPI provides their framework upon which a company or organistaion can build a banking app and serve the upi service to its customers. These apps are often constructed with adware and tracking systems built into it. The major share of UPI app is owned by google (a US based company that pioneered surveillance capitalism) followed by PhonePe app owned by Wallmart (A US retail giant).

Now imagine for a moment that every transaction you perform online weaves a story about your life. What products you purchase and how often, where do you go to buy something particular and who are the people with which you send or receive money. This information may seen surreiel at first but in long term it can be used to profile you and extract data about you at great depths. Now a random US comapany knows everything about your life and their are endless possibilities in which this data can and will be abused not only to sell your data to serve you ads back but to manipulate your very beliefs and in turn your life.

Dependency Hell

The recent developments regarding transaction fees are not suddent needs to sustain the system but was always planned to be this way. The investors poured billions into the infrastructure and banking and financial firms supported it at a loss all to ensure the population can get so deeply dependent on their system that it would be inherently impossible or atleast highly inconvineant to escape. This is the moment they have been patiently waiting for, loosing huge sums of money to be able to monopolise the system and in turn snatch away another aspect of freedom you once could easly exercise.
